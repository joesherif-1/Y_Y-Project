AI Skin Check ML experiment

Take a close-up photo of one mole or skin spot and a neural network will compare it with thousands of real dermatology photos it was trained on.

Why a mole and not your whole hand? Skin spots are the one thing a photo can genuinely show, and scientists have published big public datasets of them. Our model was trained on the HAM10000 dermatology dataset (10,015 images) — so this is a real machine-learning experiment, with its limits stated honestly below.

What this really means: the model is saying which group of its training photos your photo looks more similar to — nothing more. It has never seen you, cannot feel or measure the spot, and is wrong on a known share of test images (shown in the model info above). A dermatologist can do what no photo can. If a spot is new, growing, bleeding, or changing, get it checked regardless of what this page says.

What doctors look for (the ABCDE rule)

Asymmetry · Border irregularity · Color variation · Diameter over 6 mm · Evolving (changing over time). Any of these is a good reason to see a dermatologist — and "evolving" is something only you can notice, not an AI.
